On the North Fork, the people who make a community work are often the people who struggle most to live in it.

Think about who you count on in a given week: the firefighter and the EMT, the nurse and the home health aide, the teacher and the school counselor, the librarian, the clerk at the farm stand, the chef and the wait staff, the carpenter, the electrician, the bayman and the oyster grower.

## Between the lines

Many of these households earn between 80% and 120% of the Area Median Income. That puts them in a gap: their income is too high for most traditional affordable housing programs, yet too low to keep pace with local rents and home prices.

Housing professionals call this group the **missing middle**. We call them neighbors.

## Why it matters to everyone

When essential workers can't find attainable housing nearby, the effects ripple outward:

- Longer commutes and staffing shortages for emergency services, schools and healthcare
- Local businesses that can't stay fully staffed through the season
- Young people who grow up here but can't afford to stay
- A community that slowly loses the diversity that makes it vibrant

A vibrant, diverse, thriving and sustainable community relies on people. The missing middle is our present and our future.
